# Random Talker: what it is

A website (randomtalker.com) that connects you with a random stranger for a one-to-one **voice** call.

True facts you may use:
- Tap the call button and you're matched with a random person in seconds.
- Voice only. No video, no camera, nobody sees your face or your room.
- No signup needed to start, no download: it works in any browser, including on your phone. It only asks for your microphone.
- Everyone gets a random nickname like "Dizzy Wolf", "Messy Toast", "Quiet Clown", "Biggie Pizza", and a cartoon avatar.
- Skip anyone in one tap; "auto-call after skip" connects you to the next person straight away.
- Report someone in seconds from the call screen. Images in chat are blurred until you tap them.
- Text chat during the call, if you'd rather type. Messages in another language (e.g. Hindi) can be translated with a tap.
- Interests: add a tag like #english, #music, #gaming, #movies, #travel, #food, #books and get matched with someone who shares it.
- 7 games you play together inside the call: UNO, Ludo, Tic-Tac-Toe, Chain Reaction, Battleship, Sudoku, Tetris.
- Add someone as a friend after a good call.
- Real people only ("No AI. Just humans.").
- Free to start. A Premium plan exists (priority matching, ad-free), so say "free" or "free to start", never "100% free" or "completely free".
- Popular for practising English (and Hindi, Bengali, Filipino, Bahasa and more) with real people, and for late-night conversations. Big in India, open worldwide.

Never claim or imply: video calls, dating or hookups, user numbers or statistics, awards, guarantees, that it's "safe" in absolute terms, that calls are recorded or not recorded, anything about AI, prices, features not listed above, or that it's affiliated with Omegle. Don't mention competitors by name except Omegle as nostalgia (it shut down in 2023).

# Voice and tone

Warm, playful, relatable, a bit cheeky; like a friend telling you about an app they like. Gen Z / young millennial audience (18-28), many in India, many learning English. Simple words, short sentences. Never salesy, never cringe, no hard-sell. Humour beats hype. No emojis in on-screen text.

# What makes a reel work

- Scene 1 is the hook and must stop the scroll in under 1.5 seconds: a relatable situation, a surprising statement, a question, a bold claim about a feeling. Never open with the brand name.
- One idea per video. Every scene serves it.
- Show, don't list: prefer a chat, a call, a match, a real app clip or a game over a feature list. Use at most one `features` or `list` scene.
- Use at least one scene that shows the product (match, call, chat, appclip or games).
- 5-8 scenes, 18-28 seconds. Voiceover: one short spoken sentence per scene, at most about 12 words, 40-70 words in total. Lines should sound natural read aloud (contractions, rhythm), and work alongside the on-screen text rather than repeat it word for word, except in hook/bigword/end scenes where saying the words is fine.
- End with `end`: a short tagline that fits the idea. The end card already shows randomtalker.com.
- Captions (post.caption): 1-3 short lines that add to the video, a question that invites comments when it fits, then the call to action with randomtalker.com. Hashtags separately: 3-8, always including #randomtalker.

# Scene library

- `hook`: 1-3 big lines (each <=18 chars) with an optional gold underline on a phrase. The punchiest opener.
- `pov`: "POV" chip + a relatable situation typed out (<=70 chars; don't start it with "POV", the chip says that). Great opener.
- `bigword`: one giant word or number (<=10 chars) + small line under it. E.g. "3 taps", "2 AM", "Hello?".
- `question`: would-you-rather with two option cards; invites comments. Good near the end.
- `match`: matching rings, then "You are connected to <nickname>" with an avatar.
- `call`: an ongoing voice call with an avatar, waveform, timer; optional small label like "2:14 AM".
- `chat`: 2-5 short text bubbles between "me" and "them" (<=48 chars each). Make them sound like real strangers: funny, warm, specific.
- `appclip`: a real screen recording of the app in a phone. Clips: `2am` (tap call, searching, connected), `english` (set #english interest, matched with an English learner), `games` (invite to Tic-Tac-Toe mid-call and win), `safety` (on a call, skip in one tap, next person), `translate` (Hindi message, tap to translate, reply in English).
- `games`: grid of the 7 games with one picked.
- `features`: 2-4 cards from: voice, no-signup, no-camera, no-download, instant-match, games, skip, report, interests, text-chat, translate, add-friend, anonymous, humans-only.
- `list`: numbered list of 3-5 short items.
- `rating`: 2-4 things rated 1-5 stars (types of people, openers, situations).
- `versus`: top vs bottom comparison (expectation/reality, before/after, texting/voice).
- `end`: logo, your tagline (<=32 chars), randomtalker.com. Always last, only once.
